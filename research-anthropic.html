Anthropic: Pricing the
Pre-IPO AI Leader

How do you price a company that has no stock ticker? A valuation study of Anthropic written after it filed confidentially for an IPO.

Suhaas Mandava · 07/25/2026 · 10 pages · 9 sources

Open the PDF Download

Overview

Anthropic is a strange company to research, because you cannot buy stock in it. It is still private. In June 2026 it filed confidential paperwork with the SEC for an IPO, which got me curious about how a company gets priced before it has a ticker, and whether the number attached to it right now actually makes sense.

My argument: Anthropic's revenue growth and cost efficiency are real and well supported, but its valuation has grown faster than either one. The price investors are paying is currently ahead of what the business has actually proven.

Key points

$61.5B → $965B

The valuation ran 15.7x in 14 months

Across four rounds from March 2025 to May 2026, selling the same core product into the same market. What changed was not the business, it was what investors were willing to pay for it.

1,000+

Enterprise demand looks real

Customers spending over $1M a year roughly doubled from about 500 in two months, and eight of the ten largest companies by revenue run significant workloads on Claude. That is deep integration, not a trial.

~60%

Costs are better than expected

Compute runs near 60 percent of revenue against an estimated 75 percent plus at OpenAI, cash burn is guided down from $5.6B to about $3B, and the company projected an unusually early profitable quarter.

Ahead of proof

But the multiple outran the fundamentals

A 15.7x jump is larger than revenue growth alone justifies, so part of that number is a future that has not happened yet. The IPO filing is the real test, when public markets can check these figures against full disclosures instead of investor projections.
